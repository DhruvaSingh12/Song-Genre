PROJECT OVERVIEW:
-----------------
This repository contains a full end-to-end Music Genre Classification pipeline:
1. Playlist & Song Acquisition: YouTube audio scraping via yt-dlp.
2. Audio Preprocessing: 10-second segment clipping (10-200s duration, up to 10 
   segments per track) at 22,050 Hz 16-bit mono WAV.
3. Feature Engineering: Extraction of 31 handcrafted acoustic features 
   (Time-domain, Statistical amplitude, Spectral, and Cepstral/MFCCs) saved to CSV.
4. Spectrogram Engineering: Extraction of 128-band Log-Mel Spectrogram arrays 
   saved as .npy matrices for Computer Vision architectures.
5. Multi-Class Modeling:
   - Tabular Models: Linear SVM, Random Forest, PyTorch Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), LightGBM.
   - Deep CNN Models: MobileNetV2, EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-18 with SpecAugment & Fine-Tuning.
   - Multi-Modal Ensemble: Soft-voting ensemble combining all 7 models + full-track segment aggregation.
6. Target Classes (9 Genres): 
   country, edm, hiphop, jazz, kpop, lofi, pop, r&b, rock.

DATASET SPLIT & SPECIFICATIONS:
-------------------------------
- Sampling Rate (SR): 22,050 Hz (Mono)
- FFT Window Length (N_FFT): 2,048 samples (~92.88 ms)
- Hop Length (HOP_LENGTH): 512 samples (~23.22 ms)
- Frequency Range: 0 Hz to 8,000 Hz
- Total Dataset Instances: 16,261 audio segments
- Split Strategy: Track-level Stratified Splitting (prevents segment data leakage)
  * Train Set: 12,208 rows (75%)
  * Validation Set: 2,442 rows (15%)
  * Test Set: 1,610 rows (10%)
- Feature Dimension: 31 numerical acoustic features
- Metadata Columns: 2 ('Filename', 'Genre')
- Total CSV Columns: 33

3. DETAILED ACOUSTIC FEATURE DESCRIPTIONS & FORMULAS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. TIME-DOMAIN & AMPLITUDE STATISTICAL FEATURES (Features 1 - 9)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Zero_Crossing_Rate (ZCR)
   - Category: Time-Domain
   - Computation: Mean value of librosa.feature.zero_crossing_rate across frames 
     (frame_length=2048, hop_length=512).
   - Description: Measures the rate at which the audio signal changes its sign 
     from positive to negative or vice versa. High ZCR typically correlates with 
     noisy, percussive, unvoiced sound components (e.g., hi-hats, distorted guitars 
     in rock/metal), whereas low ZCR indicates tonal and harmonic instruments 
     (e.g., acoustic bass, piano in jazz/lo-fi).

2. RMS_Energy (Root Mean Square Energy)
   - Category: Time-Domain
   - Computation: Mean value of librosa.feature.rms across frames 
     (frame_length=2048, hop_length=512).
   - Description: Represents the effective energy and perceived loudness of the 
     audio waveform over time. Differentiates heavily compressed, high-energy 
     genres (EDM, Pop, Rock) from acoustic or soft dynamics (Lo-Fi, Jazz, Country).

3. Amplitude_Envelope_Mean
   - Category: Time-Domain
   - Computation: Mean of maximum absolute amplitudes over non-overlapping/sliding 
     windows of length 1,024 samples with hop length 512.
   - Description: Captures the average amplitude peak across the 10-second segment, 
     reflecting transient dynamics, compression levels, and peak punchiness.

4. Energy_Entropy
   - Category: Time-Domain
   - Computation: Short-term energy entropy computed across 10 equal sub-slices of 
     the 10-second segment:
       probs = energies / sum(energies)
       Entropy = -sum(probs * log2(probs + 1e-12))
   - Description: Quantifies abrupt changes and transitions in audio energy over 
     time. A uniform energy distribution produces high entropy (steady EDM beats 
     or wall-of-sound rock), while sparse or intermittent musical phrases yield 
     lower entropy.

5. Mean_Amplitude
   - Category: Amplitude-Statistic
   - Computation: np.mean(y) of raw audio samples.
   - Description: Represents the DC offset and central tendency of the raw signal. 
     Ideally near zero in properly mastered recordings; subtle variations capture 
     asymmetric waveforms or mastering artifacts.

6. Variance_Amplitude
   - Category: Amplitude-Statistic
   - Computation: np.var(y) of raw audio samples.
   - Description: Measures the statistical spread of the sample amplitudes around 
     the mean. A higher variance corresponds to larger instantaneous volume 
     deviations and high signal power.

7. Skewness_Amplitude
   - Category: Amplitude-Statistic
   - Computation: Third standardized moment: ((y - mean)^3).mean() / (std^3).
   - Description: Measures the asymmetry of the amplitude distribution. Skewness 
     differs between symmetrical sinusoids and asymmetric transient audio pulses 
     (such as sharp synth plucks or brass onsets).

8. Kurtosis_Amplitude
   - Category: Amplitude-Statistic
   - Computation: Fourth standardized moment: ((y - mean)^4).mean() / (var^2).
   - Description: Quantifies the "tailedness" and sharpness of the amplitude 
     distribution. Heavy-tailed distributions (high kurtosis) indicate sparse, 
     spiky transients (isolated kick drums, rimshots in hip-hop/jazz), whereas 
     low kurtosis reflects sustained, saturated tones.

9. Peak_to_Peak_Amplitude
   - Category: Amplitude-Statistic
   - Computation: np.ptp(y) = np.max(y) - np.min(y).
   - Description: Full dynamic peak-to-peak range between the highest positive 
     and lowest negative sample values. Reflects head-room, dynamic range 
     compression, and mastering limiting.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. SPECTRAL-DOMAIN FEATURES (Features 10 - 18)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Extracted via Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) with n_fft=2048, hop_length=512.

10. Spectral_Centroid
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: librosa.feature.spectral_centroid(S=S, sr=22050).
    - Description: Center of mass of the frequency spectrum. Corresponds directly 
      to the perceived "brightness" or "sharpness" of the sound. Bright genres 
      with prominent high frequencies (EDM, Pop, Rock) exhibit significantly higher 
      centroids than mellow genres (Jazz, Lo-Fi, R&B).

11. Spectral_Bandwidth
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: librosa.feature.spectral_bandwidth(S=S, sr=22050).
    - Description: Measures the spectral spread (standard deviation) of frequency 
      components around the spectral centroid. Broad, multi-layered arrangements 
      (EDM, Rock) have wide bandwidths, while minimalist acoustic tracks exhibit 
      narrower bandwidths.

12. Spectral_Contrast
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: librosa.feature.spectral_contrast(S=S, sr=22050).
    - Description: Evaluates the energy difference between spectral peaks (harmonic 
      components) and valleys (ambient noise/sub-bands). High contrast implies clear, 
      distinct harmonic notes (classical, clean acoustic guitars in country), whereas 
      low contrast denotes dense, continuous noise or heavy distortion.

13. Spectral_Rolloff
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: librosa.feature.spectral_rolloff(S=S, sr=22050, roll_percent=0.85).
    - Description: Frequency below which 85% of total spectral energy is contained. 
      Helps distinguish bass-dominated genres (Hip-Hop, Lo-Fi) from treble-dominated 
      genres (Country, Rock, Pop).

14. Spectral_Flatness
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: librosa.feature.spectral_flatness(S=S).
    - Description: Quantifies whether the spectrum is tone-like or noise-like. 
      Computed as the ratio of geometric mean to arithmetic mean of spectral energy. 
      High flatness indicates noisy, broadband sound (white noise, heavy cymbals); 
      low flatness indicates isolated harmonic peaks (melodic vocals, flute, brass).

15. Spectral_Flux
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: librosa.onset.onset_strength(S=S).
    - Description: Measure of the local rate of spectral change between successive 
      frames. Directly reflects rhythmic tempo, beat onsets, and percussive punch. 
      Very high in EDM and Hip-Hop; lower in smooth ambient or jazz tracks.

16. Spectral_Skewness
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: 3rd spectral moment: sum(((freqs - mu) / sigma)^3 * mag_norm).
    - Description: Measures the asymmetry of the frequency distribution. Positive 
      skewness indicates energy concentrated in lower frequencies (bass/mid-range) 
      with a long tail extending into higher frequencies.

17. Spectral_Kurtosis
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: 4th spectral moment: sum(((freqs - mu) / sigma)^4 * mag_norm).
    - Description: Measures the peakedness of the spectral shape. High spectral 
      kurtosis characterizes spectra dominated by a few narrow harmonic tones; 
      low kurtosis indicates evenly distributed frequency bands.

18. Spectral_Slope
    - Category: Spectral-Domain
    - Computation: Linear regression slope of the decibel magnitude spectrum over 
      frequency bins.
    - Description: Rate of spectral roll-off and decay as frequency increases. 
      Warm, darker genres exhibit steep negative slopes; bright modern mixes show 
      gentler slopes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. MEL-FREQUENCY CEPSTRAL COEFFICIENTS (Features 19 - 31)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Extracted via librosa.feature.mfcc(y=y, sr=22050, n_mfcc=13) and averaged over time.

19. MFCC_1:
    - Overall acoustic energy and average spectral amplitude across mel bands.
20. MFCC_2:
    - Balance between low-frequency and high-frequency content (spectral tilt).
21. MFCC_3:
    - Curvature of spectral shape; distinguishes nasal/vocal and string resonance.
22. MFCC_4:
    - Sub-band balance; sensitive to presence of electric guitar distortion and punch.
23. MFCC_5:
    - Formant structure representation; distinguishes male/female vocals and synths.
24. MFCC_6:
    - Acoustic warmth and mid-range brass/acoustic guitar presence.
25. MFCC_7:
    - High-mid harmonic presence and percussive snare snap.
26. MFCC_8:
    - Higher harmonic formant resonance and vocal articulation.
27. MFCC_9:
    - Fine timbral texture; distinguishes woodwinds, clean strings, and synth pads.
28. MFCC_10:
    - High-frequency air band and harmonic overtones.
29. MFCC_11:
    - Cymbal shimmer and sibilance energy distribution.
30. MFCC_12:
    - Fine-grained spectral envelope resonance.
31. MFCC_13:
    - High-order cepstral envelope coefficient for subtle genre-specific timbre.

   country, and r&b all exceeding 90% F1-score.
